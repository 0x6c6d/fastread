# T031: Player effective wpm and drift-free schedule

Phase 3 · Model: sonnet: fake-clock accounting on an existing model, no I/O or security decision (routing policy example) · Depends-on: T030

## Brief
Add effective-wpm accounting (R27) to `state.Player` and prove the absolute-deadline schedule
does not drift (§5 timing accuracy). Package `internal/state`, module
`github.com/0x6c6d/fastread`. Existing API (keep): `NewPlayer(Config{Tokens, Start, WPM, Size,
ShowProgress, Clock})`, `Start()`, `Tick() (finished bool)`, `Apply(a Action) (quit bool)`
with `ActTogglePause` (pause keeps `remaining`, resume sets `deadline = now + remaining`),
`ActNext/ActPrev/ActHome` jumps, `Deadline()`, `Index()`, `Finished()`; `Tick` advances with
`deadline = previous deadline + timing.DelayPara(text, wpm, paraEnd)` and may skip several
words when called late; the fake clock type in `player_test.go`.

Add to `internal/state/player.go`:
```go
// EffectiveWPM returns words completed per minute of unpaused playing time, rounded to the
// nearest integer. ok is false before Start, while fewer than 2 words were completed, or
// when no unpaused time has elapsed (the UI then shows "—").
func (p *Player) EffectiveWPM() (wpm int, ok bool)
```
Accounting:
- *Completed words*: incremented once for every word whose deadline passes inside `Tick`
  (each index advance, and the final word when `Tick` reports finished). Words skipped by
  Next/Prev/Home jumps are not counted.
- *Unpaused time*: from `Start()` on, time spent playing; pausing stops it, resuming
  restarts it; it stops for good when the player finishes (at the `Now()` of the finishing
  `Tick`). Evaluated at `Clock.Now()` when `EffectiveWPM` is called.
- value = completed / unpaused.Minutes(), `math.Round`.

Tests in `internal/state/player_test.go` (fake clock only, no sleeps):
- `TestEffectiveWPM`, tokens: 10 words `aa` (last `ParaEnd`), 300 wpm (200 ms each), Start at
  t0. Check in order: before Start → ok false; Tick at t0+200ms → completed 1 → ok false;
  Ticks at t0+400ms, t0+600ms, t0+800ms → (300, true); pause at t0+800ms; clock to t0+10.8s →
  still (300, true); resume at t0+10.8s; Tick at t0+11.0s → (300, true); clock to t0+11.1s
  (no Tick) → (273, true) (5 words in 1.1 s); a Next jump while playing adds nothing to
  completed. A second table row at 600 wpm with 6 plain words and no `ParaEnd` at all
  (100 ms each), ticked exactly at deadlines, ends with (600, true) after finishing, and the value stays the same when the clock moves
  on 1 minute after the finish.
- `TestScheduleNoDrift`: 100 tokens cycling through `word`, `word,`, `word.`,
  `extraordinarily`, `naïve`, with `ParaEnd` on every 10th and on the last; 600 wpm;
  Start at t0. Loop: set the clock to `Deadline() + jitter` where jitter cycles through
  0, 3, 7, 9 ms (always less than the shortest delay), then `Tick()`; until finished. Assert
  after every Tick that `Deadline() == t0 + sum of DelayPara over tokens[0..Index()]`
  (absolute, exact: late Ticks never shift later deadlines) and at the end that `Finished()`
  is true, `Index() == 99`, and the final deadline equals `t0 + Σ DelayPara(all 100)`
  exactly (well within the spec's ±5%). Second subtest: one Tick arriving 300 ms after a
  deadline advances more than one word in that single call, and the schedule stays anchored
  to t0 (same sum assertion after it and at the end).

Constraints: change only `internal/state/player.go` and `internal/state/player_test.go`; no
goroutines, no I/O, no real clock; all earlier `TestPlayer*` tests keep passing.
